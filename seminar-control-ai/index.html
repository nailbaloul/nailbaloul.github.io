Talk 01

Adnan Saood

Robotics Engineer and PhD student, ENSTA — U2IS

Touch for robotics

Physical interaction is a fundamental dimension of human communication, conveying social and affective information that speech and vision alone cannot fully capture. Touch is also inherently action-dependent, as the way we interact with objects shapes the information we perceive about them. These properties motivate a broader role for touch in human-robot interaction (HRI), extending beyond sensing towards communication, perception, and reasoning. This talk presents ongoing research exploring two complementary dimensions of tactile intelligence in robotics. The first focuses on social touch. We introduce a pneumatic soft-skin interface integrated into a humanoid robot's torso, reproducing physiological rhythms such as breathing and heartbeat for affective expression. Experimental results demonstrate improved recognition of the robot's emotional states and highlight the potential of this interface for guided breathing exercises. We also present a modular tactile glove comprising 90 sensors, which, combined with kinematic data, enables a robot to characterize its human partner during handshakes. Our findings highlight the importance of gentle grip, arm compliance, and motion synchrony in shaping human perceptions of physical interaction with robots. The second part addresses tactile perception and interaction with the environment. We introduce FRACTAL, a generative, factorized model of action-conditioned tactile affordances built around a latent interaction regime. Unlike approaches that treat affordances as fixed properties of locations or surfaces, FRACTAL models them as expected outcomes conditioned on the robot's actions and interaction dynamics. This provides a structured framework for interpreting tactile observations in relation to the actions that generate them. Together, these contributions explore how touch can enable robots not only to communicate and interact socially with humans, but also to perceive and reason about the physical world.

Talk 02

Pauline Bernard

Associate Professor, Mines Paris – PSL, CAS

Provable state estimation: from KKL to recurrent models

Estimating the state of a dynamical system from observations is a crucial task in robotics and control. Despite their empirical success, recurrent models often lack formal guarantees on convergence and robustness. We bridge this gap by analyzing these architectures from the perspective of nonlinear observer theory, and in particular KKL observers. By framing RNNs as a time-discretization of continuous latent dynamical systems (Neural ODEs), we demonstrate that enforcing a contraction condition on the latent flow guarantees the existence of a smooth, injective map of the true physical state into the latent space. We show that this architecture corresponds to KKL observers with nonlinear latent dynamics and acts as a tunable observer: by increasing a single scalar gain that scales the latent vector field, the estimation error can be driven to an arbitrarily small neighborhood of zero at an exponentially fast rate. We further establish quantitative bounds on the MLP complexity required to decode this latent state, and extend our formal guarantees to the discrete-time residual RNN implementation. We show that on a real mobile robotic task, a provably contracting GRU model outperforms a vanilla GRU with substantially more stable training. We also report that unconstrained GRUs converge to contracting dynamics on this task, suggesting that contraction is an emergent property of well-trained sequence models.
